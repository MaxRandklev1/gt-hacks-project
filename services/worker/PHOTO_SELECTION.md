# Local training-photo selection

`select_best_photos(paths)` takes exactly eight local image paths and returns all eight records in input order. Each record has a zero-based `index`, `selected`, numeric `score`, human-readable `reason`, and JSON-safe `metrics`. Exactly five distinct usable photos are selected. If fewer qualify, a `ValueError` explains which photos to replace before submitting all eight again. The worker should show these reasons and stop before GPU training on failure.

The selector applies EXIF orientation, checks that a file is a readable still image, and rejects images below 256 pixels on either side. OpenCV's bundled frontal-face Haar detector must find exactly one face at least 96 pixels across. Extremely blurred/detail-free or heavily under/overexposed faces are rejected. Remaining images are scored for face resolution, sharpness measured at a normalized crop size, and preservation of unclipped detail. Byte-equivalent images and conservative perceptual-hash/thumbnail near matches are deduplicated, retaining the stronger photo.

Selection starts with quality, then allows a small bonus for different face framing, brightness bands, and horizontal placement. This is a deterministic heuristic: it does not estimate pose, verify that the photos show the same person, rank attractiveness, or establish universal fairness. Brightness bands can reflect skin tone or surroundings as well as illumination. Exposure scoring penalizes clipping rather than favoring a particular normal skin brightness.

The frontal detector can miss profiles, occluded faces, unusual lighting, or faces outside its training distribution; its rejection does not prove that no person is present. It can also produce false positives. Prefer sharp, unfiltered, single-person photos with a clear face, and manually review the five selected images. Duplicate detection can miss crops/major edits or occasionally flag similar distinct shots. Quality scores are relative screening signals, not predictions of training quality. Tests use generated synthetic pixels and mocked face boxes; they verify selection behavior, not real-world detector accuracy.

Runtime dependencies are Pillow, NumPy, and `opencv-python-headless` (whose package includes the Haar classifier). Imports are lazy, no network requests or model downloads are made, and the selector neither starts training nor alters the input files.
